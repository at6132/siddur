What is an eruv?

On Shabbos, Jewish law prohibits transferring objects between a private domain and a public domain, and carrying within a public domain. An eruv is a boundary — using existing walls, fences, and specially prepared poles and wires — that halachically merges an area into one private domain, permitting carrying within it on Shabbos and Yom Kippur.

The Cincinnati Eruv encloses the neighborhoods where our community lives, so families can carry keys, bring food to one another, and push strollers and wheelchairs to shul on Shabbos.

Even with an eruv, it is important that we and our children learn the laws of carrying on Shabbos — the eruv's validity can never be fully guaranteed, and not every community is enclosed by one.

Rabbinic oversight

The eruv is endorsed by Rabbi Moshe Heinemann (administrator of Star-K), who conducts a personal survey roughly every ten years, under the oversight of the Cincinnati Beis Din (Rabbi Ezra Goldschmiedt, Rabbi Stuart Lavenda, Rabbi David Spetner & Rabbi Avrohom Weinrib).

Good to know

Can I always rely on the eruv?

The eruv is checked regularly, but its status can change due to weather, construction, or utility work. Validity can never be fully guaranteed — check the status above (or call the hotline) before relying on it each Shabbos.

What about apartment buildings?

The eruv works for residents of apartment buildings. That said, buildings with two or more Jewish households should also make a small eruv of their own (without a blessing) — a backup that keeps the building's common areas covered in the unlikely event the community eruv is down. It is far simpler to make than the communal one; your rav can help.

Does the eruv permit everything on Shabbos?

No. The eruv only addresses the prohibition of carrying. All other Shabbos laws and the sanctity of the day remain fully in force. Athletic activities in particular often involve other prohibitions and run counter to the restful spirit of Shabbos — discuss specific activities with your rav.

How do I get status alerts?

Weekly status is posted to the CincinnatiShuls listserv, and a hotline is available at (513) 351-3788. To join the automated call list, email [email protected].

Is this a "good eruv"? Why don't some people use it?

Much effort has gone into satisfying a number of relatively strict minority opinions, and the eruv certainly meets the standard of mainstream Orthodox communities. Still, no eruv can meet every standard, and some choose not to rely on it. Those who use the eruv and those who don't should respect each other's approach — so that the eruv truly unifies our community.

We just had a major storm — is the eruv still up?

In all the years of the eruv's existence, bad weather has never invalidated it. After the weekly check, you may assume the eruv is up, even during or following a storm.
